Station by station

In the real lab test, you walk from station to station. At each one you name a mineral in hand specimen or under the microscope, or you say what a sample means. This page lets you practise that. Each station has one photo, a clock, and gold for every right answer on the first try.

How this works

Your simulation photos (10 Nov) are the photos you took in the lab revision class with Ian: hand specimens (Part A), microscope fields (Part B) and ore samples (Part C). The answers are your own labels and notes from that class. After a Part C answer you see your notes, facts added from the lectures, and a "Check" list: those are my reading of a photo or note, not Ian’s words.

Every run mixes the station types evenly, so each run includes microscope stations. To practise only the microscope, tap Microscope only. "Microscope: name the mineral" shows an ore mineral in a polished section under plane-polarised reflected light, and you name it. The mineral name on the photo is cropped off. You can use the bench tools as in the lab: cross the polars, test hardness with the needle, or try the magnet. Each tool you use costs 1 oz at that station, because in the real test it costs you time. These photos come from the Lecture 4 slides. There is one photo for each of 19 ore minerals, so you will see the same photos again. That is fine: learn the features, not the photo.

Mineral hand specimen stations use the lab's own mineral set (to learn the tests behind each name, play the Mineral Sudoku): name the mineral from the specimen (or from its streak). Distractors come mostly from the same mineral class, so it's quartz against opal and calcite against magnesite, not quartz against galena. Alteration stations use the lab's alteration examples, with the specimens photographed beside a printed label left out.

Ore suite hand specimens are the lab's deposit samples: say what kind of deposit each came from. Once you answer, you get the specimen's code and, where the code matches, its line from the lab's Ore Deposit Specimen Descriptions sheet. Specimens with a deposit or mine name written on them are left out. After every answer, right or wrong, you also see why the deposit belongs to its group (from the specimen sheet and the lecture), how to recognise that group, and, if you picked another group, what that group would look like and how to tell the two apart.

Ore suite stations show a polished block from the lab collection under reflected light, from deposits like Ok Tedi, Rosebery, Sudbury, Kalgoorlie and Mount Kelly, and ask what kind of deposit it came from, using the deposit groups the lab material uses. The file names photographed onto the images are cropped off so they can't give the answer away.

Field task stations are the "observe first, then interpret" tasks from every lecture page. Describe what you see and say what it means, out loud or on paper, the way you would at a lab bench, then show the answer and rate yourself honestly. A match on the main point pays 2 oz and builds the combo; "not quite" sends it to your revisit list. If the clock runs out, the answer just opens. Lectures 5 onward also add their photo quizzes (vein types, mine faces and the Sn-W hand specimens) to the drill core and mineral stations.

A right answer first time pays 3 oz, plus 1 oz if you answer in the first third of the clock. Answers in a row build a combo that multiplies the gold. A wrong answer or a timeout breaks it, and the station goes to the end-of-run list with the correct answer and a link back to the lecture section that teaches it. The stations you've missed before come up more often.

This is practice in the spirit of the real test, not a copy of it. The real one uses actual specimens and slides from the lab classes, and it covers hand-specimen minerals that these pages don't have photos of yet. More stations arrive as each lecture page is built.
